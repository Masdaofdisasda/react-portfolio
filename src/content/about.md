Hi, I'm **David Köppl** — a software engineer and master’s student in **Visual Computing**, currently based in Vienna. I’m deeply passionate about **graphics programming, engine architecture, and low-level GPU work**, and I've spent the past few years building systems from the ground up — not just to make things work, but to understand *why* they work.

## What I’m Working On

My main personal project is a **custom Vulkan-based game engine** written in C++. It supports modern rendering features like **descriptor buffers**, **mesh and task shaders**, and **ray-traced shadows**, along with a custom **atmospheric sky simulation**. I built the architecture myself — complete with design diagrams, a **frame graph system** for automatic resource synchronization, and a fully CMake/CPM-managed build pipeline. Through this project, I’ve learned a ton about **memory management**, **render graph orchestration**, and the nuts and bolts of linking and interface design in C++.

Alongside that, I’ve worked on a **high-performance particle simulation** that behaves like fluid — simulating **over 1 million particles in real time** inside the browser. It’s mobile-friendly and uses machine learning to control particle behavior dynamically.

## Research & Academic Work

My **bachelor thesis** focused on improving light optimization techniques in differentiable rendering. The algorithm I developed ended up outperforming the previous standard used by my advisor, which was a rewarding moment — even if the technique is now being superseded. I’m also involved in a **research project on compressing light data** for real-time rendering using neural networks (though most of that work is currently confidential).

## Side Projects

I enjoy mixing art and tech — like in my **browser-based music visualizer**, which reacts to microphone input and creates beat-synced, animated visuals. It was designed for live projections onto glass surfaces and runs smoothly across desktops and mobile browsers.
